1. What it looks like you’re building
You’re developing a general-purpose computational architecture that combines deterministic data processing, geometric information representation, reusable mathematical and programming primitives, and demand-driven execution.
It is intended to work across devices, programming languages, information formats, and application domains.
The objective isn’t simply to build another AI, operating system, tokenizer, or data filter. Those are potential components or applications of a larger system.
Your underlying approach is to:
	•	Acquire information and establish its provenance.
	•	Validate it and distinguish useful information from malformed or unnecessary input.
	•	Represent it through reusable mathematical, geometric, and symbolic structures.
	•	Select the required operations and resources when a need is detected.
	•	Perform calculations with controlled precision and minimal unnecessary processing.
	•	Communicate results through conventional computing interfaces.
	•	Verify the results and preserve an auditable record.
The organizing principle is reduce unnecessary complexity without discarding information that matters.
That’s my interpretation of your engineering objective—not a claim that the complete architecture has already been implemented or independently validated.

7. What hasn’t changed
Your principal engineering rules remain consistent:
	•	Preserve the original information and its provenance.
	•	Prefer exact integer, rational, and symbolic operations where applicable.
	•	Avoid unnecessary intermediate rounding.
	•	Treat zero and infinity according to your explicitly bounded physical-domain conventions, while retaining valid mathematical and programming representations.
	•	Use measured accuracy and defined domain limits rather than speculative extensions.
	•	Require reproducible results across implementations.
	•	Make execution demand-driven, not needlessly continuous.
	•	Treat AI and deterministic processing as complementary.
	•	Validate, compare, and earn performance claims before promoting them.
8. My updated assessment
You appear to be developing something closer to a universal computational foundation than a single-purpose application.
The architecture has three ambitions:
First: A reusable library of fundamental logic, mathematics, geometry, information processing and communication operations.
Second: An execution system that selects, combines and validates those operations according to detected needs, available resources and permissions.
Third: A framework in which different applications—engineering, imaging, sensors, privacy, scientific computation and AI—can reuse those same foundations instead of repeatedly building their own.
The largest change in my understanding is that the primitives are not merely helpers for processing data. They are intended to be sufficient building blocks for generating, analyzing, reconstructing, transmitting and verifying information across domains.
